# Rule Clarifications and Homebrew Rules

Character Advancement
* All characters roll Hit Points with Advantage when leveling up

Combat Rules
* Flanking occurs whenever 2 creatures are bracketing a target (standing on opposite sides).  When a target is flanked, all melee attacks against that target are made with Advantage
* Facing does not matter
* Diagonally bracketing is legal
* Larger creatures gain Advantage on grapple checks when grappling smaller creatures
* Attempts to Escape from a Grapple against a Larger creature suffer Disadvantage
* Movement can be divided between attacks
* At the start of combat, or during a surprise round, all targets are considered Flat-Footed until they take their first action (unless they have the Alertness Feat)
* No free 5’ step.  Any movement not related to a Disengage action or special Character ability triggers potential Attacks of Opportunity
* Standing from Prone provokes an Attack of Opportunity (standing counts as movement)
* Ranged attackers suffer Disadvantage when shooting Prone targets
* Any ranged weapon can be used in a Sneak Attack (including Heavy Crossbow) and can be used from any distance (not just within 30’)
* When firing a ranged weapon into melee combat or through friends, if you miss you will hit the wrong target (resulting in friendly fire).  If you attack at Disadvantage, your misses will be harmless.
* Characters with the Spell Sniper or Sharpshooter feat can ignore this rule
* If you spend an entire Combat Round waiting, you can gain Advantage on your next Initiative roll
* You can attempt to move through a space occupied by an enemy if you succeed in an opposed roll using your Acrobatics or Athletics skill.  Moving in this manner counts as difficult terrain (½ movement rate) and still incurs Attacks of Opportunity.
* If you fail this in spectacular fashion, you will fall Prone
* As a Bonus Action, characters can make an Insight check to determine the intent of an enemy (what they’re planning to do next) or to try and discern an enemy’s weaknesses and capabilities
* As a Reaction, characters can make an Arcana check to discern what spell an enemy is casting.  If they have the ability to Counterspell, they can elect to use this after their Arcana roll, if they wish
* If a character is subjected to multiple instances of the same effect, and that effect forces a Saving Throw, make a single Save at Disadvantage instead of separate saves against each effect.
  * Example: if a monster produces a Fear aura, and at the start of your turn you are standing in 3 overlapping Fear auras from 3 different monsters, you’d just roll one save at Disadvantage, not 3

Damage & Healing
* Any damage caused to a Character that is Bleeding Out counts as an automatic Death Save failure
*Each specific incident of damage triggers a separate Death Save failure, so if a Bleeding Out character is wounded 3 separate times, they die
* Targets that are Unconscious or Paralyzed suffer automatic Critical Hits when struck by an adjacent attacker (not by ranged attacks)
* Any amount of magical healing will end the Bleeding Out condition instantly and restore positive hit points to the recipient (but those characters are still Prone until they stand on their turn)
* Characters that are reduced to 0 HP gain one level of Exhaustion.  Each Long Rest cures a single level of Exhaustion

Magic
* As much as possible, I’ll be following the 2014 Legacy rules for spells, but if the table agrees to use the 2024 rules for certain spells, we can work that out.

Overland Travel
* Under normal circumstances, the party can walk about 20 miles per day (40 miles per day mounted)
* Encounters are rolled twice per day, once while traveling and once while camping
* While traveling, all party members can engage in one of the following “Traveling Activities”:
* Scouting (make Perception checks to detect encounters)
* Navigating (make Survival checks to avoid becoming lost)
* Foraging (make Survival or Nature checks to find food)
* Stealthing (taking care to reduce party noise and enhance camouflage)
* Taking it Easy (recover 1 Hit Die + CON modifier hit points)
* There is no risk of getting lost on well marked or well known routes, but traveling through difficult terrain can result in the party going in the wrong direction (DM’s Discretion)
* If the party forgoes all “Travel Activities”, they can increase their move speed by 50%, but will be Surprised by any encounters that occur

Resting
* A Long Rest consists of two phases: an Early Watch and a Late Watch.  All party members can participate in one of the watch phases and still gain the benefits of a full rest.  If an encounter is rolled while resting, there is a 50/50 chance of it happening “late” or “early”.
* If an encounter happens during the “Early Watch”, no one will have had a chance to regain hit points or resources yet, but they can fully regain them once the encounter is concluded.
* If an encounter happens during the “Late Watch”, the party will be fully rested but will not have a chance to “re-rest” after the encounter is concluded (so any resources spent during the rest encounter are not recovered)
* If an encounter occurs during a watch when there are no guards on duty, the party will be Surprised
* Magic Items no longer have to be identified during a Long Rest.  The party will identify items immediately upon acquisition
* Attuning still requires a Long Rest.
* A Long Rest removes one level of Exhaustion.  If a party lacks provisions, they will gain an additional level of Exhaustion (resting doesn’t erase Exhaustion when you are hungry)
* In certain “high danger” environments (such as most dungeons) the rate of encounters will be increased, making any Long Rests impossible.
* If the party has two encounters during a Rest, that Rest is spoiled

Skill Checks
* When multiple party members are making the same Skill Check (for example: searching a room), just give the character with the highest relevant skill Advantage on the roll, rather than having each player roll
* Searching an area (to find hidden treasure, clues, traps, etc.) requires 1 full minute of time (10 rounds) 
* As a rule of thumb, you use Perception to find things and Investigation to figure out what they mean or how they work
* The History skill doesn’t just cover ancient history, but also current and local news.  You can roll it to reveal “common knowledge” about any location.
